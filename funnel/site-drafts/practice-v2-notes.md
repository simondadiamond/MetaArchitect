# Practice v2 — three answers to "it looks empty"

Base: `sm-site-b.html` (Direction B, "The Practice"). Untouched.
Output: `practice-v2-a.html`, `practice-v2-b.html`, `practice-v2-c.html`.
Generated by `build_v2.py` — each variant is the base plus one appended treatment
stylesheet (source order beats the base at equal specificity) and, for A and C, a
handful of markup insertions. Re-runnable.

Deep petrol ground, bone type, airy rhythm, both SVG diagrams and all copy are
unchanged in all three.

---

## The diagnosis first

"Empty" was three separate faults, and it matters that they're separate because
each variant fixes them by a different route:

1. **The right channel.** Section headings are capped at 15ch inside a 1120px
   column, so roughly half the width of every section head is blank. The standfirst
   then sits *below* the heading at 52ch, leaving that blank half unaddressed.
2. **The bands don't read.** `--ground #102E33` and `--ground-2 #0C2429` differ by
   about 4% luminance. The alternation was invisible, so seven distinct sections
   read as one undifferentiated field — which is what makes generous spacing feel
   like absence rather than rhythm.
3. **The diagrams float.** Both SVGs sit on bare ground with no plane under them,
   so the hero's right column reads as a small drawing lost in a large dark area.

Nothing here is a spacing problem, which is why none of the three fixes touch
`--sect` or any line-height. All three pages are *taller* than the base, not denser.

---

## Who this had to be credible to

From `funnel/icp-v2.md`: segment #1 is a bookkeeping, accounting or tax practice of
1–20 staff in Quebec or Ontario, bilingual, professionally conservative, holding
client financial data under Law 25. The buyer signs personally. Sector consultant-use
rate 6.9%, the highest in the economy — this is a person who does hire outsiders, and
who judges them the way a professional judges another professional.

Three constraints fell out of that:

- **No tech-startup vocabulary.** No gradients-as-decoration, no glassmorphism, no
  violet, no acid accent, no isometric illustration. A visual that reads "someone's
  weekend project" costs money in this segment specifically, because the substitute
  isn't a competitor — it's "I'll keep doing it myself."
- **Segment-portable.** The ICP is explicitly unconfirmed pending the 30-day test,
  with trades as the named fallback. Nothing here may hard-commit to accounting
  iconography. No calculators, no ledgers-as-illustration, no receipt motifs. The
  references below are all *material* references (ruled paper, engraved security
  printing, brass) that survive a segment move intact.
- **The accent had to be warm, not blue.** The ui-ux-pro-max palette DB's closest
  conservative-regulated analogue (Legal Services) pairs authority navy with trust
  gold `#B45309`. Gold/brass is the received convention for regulated professional
  trust. Blue-on-dark is the fintech default and would also collide with the petrol
  ground. I took the convention and lightened it to hold contrast on a dark ground.

## A — "Ruled"

**What was added.** A ledger rule field — 1px bone hairlines at 5.5% on a 40px
rhythm — running the full page height, carried at full strength into the shaded
bands rather than tinted through them. Every section head becomes a bounded
masthead: hairlines above and below, a 74px solid bone tab at the left, and at
≥900px a two-column split that moves the standfirst into the channel that used to
be blank, divided by a vertical hairline. Both diagrams get plates — a raised panel
with a hairline border — so they sit on something. No new hue at all.

**Why it suits this buyer.** The reference is ruled accounting paper, which is the
literal material this person handles daily, and it is the only one of the three
treatments that adds zero colour risk. The masthead is how a serious print
publication handles a short headline: you don't fill the space, you bound it. The
channel now carries real copy rather than decoration.

**Trade-off.** Two, and the first is the serious one. **It doesn't answer what Simon
actually said.** He named "an accent colour or imagery"; A gives him neither and
returns more monochrome, better organised. If the page felt cold before, A makes it
colder and more austere. Second: hairline rules plus a broadsheet masthead plus zero
colour is close to a recognisable generic look — the difference here is that the base
already used hairlines as its system and the ruling is subject-derived, but it's a
thin margin.

## B — "Brass" — recommended

**What was added.**

- **One accent, `#D2A968`.** 6.6:1 on the ground, so it clears AA at any size,
  including body text.
- **A static ground wash.** Three fixed radial gradients — a warm bloom top-right,
  a cool petrol lift at mid-left, a faint warm floor — so the ground is *lit* rather
  than flat. No animation, no scroll effects.
- **A real tonal progression.** The shaded bands become vertical gradients and the
  pricing band gets its own deeper, warmer mix, so the seven sections read as seven
  planes instead of an on/off alternation.
- **The band rule.** One brass rule per section, solid for the first 60px at the
  left margin then fading across the full column to nothing. This is what answers
  fault #1: the space beside a short heading is now bounded margin, not blank.
- **Display type sized to the column** — h1 to 3.75rem, section heads to 3.5rem,
  with `text-wrap: balance` so the rags stay clean.
- **Panels lit** with a 1px inset top highlight and a deep soft drop, so shelves and
  the card read as planes rather than flat rectangles.

**The accent has a rule, and the rule is the idea.** Brass marks the human
commitments and nothing else: the published price, the guarantee (left rule plus the
"The guarantee." run-in), the approval branch and pill in the sprint diagram, and the
deliverable checkmarks. **The primary CTA stays bone deliberately** — it is already
the highest-contrast element on the page at 12.3:1 and painting it brass would drop it
to 6.6:1, weakening the one thing the page exists to get clicked. Colour goes to
meaning; contrast stays with the action.

**Why it suits this buyer.** Brass on deep petrol is the palette of a professional
office rather than a product: brass nameplate, dark panelling, cream paper. It reads
established. The single highest-value place to put warmth on this page is the
published price, because publishing the price *is* the trust argument the whole page
makes — and that's where the boldest move went. It also directly answers the ICP's
central finding, that relevance and recognition outrank price and risk-reversal: the
page now looks like it was made by someone who has done this before.

**Trade-off.** Colour is the highest-risk lever with a conservative buyer, and brass
is one bad decision away from reading either "crypto" (if it goes more saturated) or
"wedding invitation" (if it spreads to headings and body). It survives only under
severe discipline — eight uses on a page this long. If Simon likes it and asks for
"more of the gold," that is the request to refuse. Second, smaller: the ground wash
means the page can't be rendered as flat colour blocks in any future email or PDF
derivative without losing something.

## C — "Security paper"

**What was added.** Engine-turned guilloche — the concentric-rotated-ellipse rosettes
and interwoven sine bands printed on cheques, share certificates and banknotes.
Generated as real inline SVG (defined once in `<defs>`, instanced with `<use>`), held
at 5.5% opacity so it reads as watermark and engraving rather than illustration.
Five rosette placements, cropped by the section edges; a security wave band along the
top of each shaded band. Section heads get an engraved double hairline. The pricing
shelves and the card get the **certificate double frame** — a second hairline inset
7px inside the panel border. Brass appears three times only.

**Why it suits this buyer.** This is the strongest subject reference of the three:
engraved security printing is the visual language of financial instruments, and it is
essentially impossible to mistake for a startup aesthetic. It fills the blank channel
with something that means "documents, verified" rather than with an ornament. And it
is fully segment-portable — a contractor recognises a certificate too.

**Trade-off.** It is the most fragile. At 5.5% it depends on the viewer's display and
ambient light; on a cheap or badly-calibrated monitor a chunk of it disappears and C
degrades into a slightly-fussier base. Push the opacity up to compensate and it flips
from "engraved" to "decorative," which is the exact failure this buyer punishes. It
also carries the most weight (77KB vs ~35KB) and the most DOM, and the rosettes have
to be suppressed below 900px to keep the phone airy — meaning mobile gets a materially
different, weaker version of the idea.

---

## Recommendation: B

**B is the one to ship**, for three reasons in order of weight.

1. **It answers the note.** Simon named an accent colour or imagery. B is the accent
   answer executed with discipline, and it's the only one of the three where the page
   stops looking cold. A returns more monochrome; C's answer is real but is invisible
   to a viewer on the wrong screen. B works on every screen.
2. **It solves all three faults, not one.** The band rule fixes the empty channel, the
   tonal progression fixes the invisible bands, the warm pool fixes the floating
   diagram. A fixes all three too but only structurally; C fixes the channel well and
   the bands barely.
3. **It buys the most credibility per unit of risk.** Brass is the received convention
   for regulated professional trust, so it reads as competent rather than as taste. And
   putting the accent on the price and the guarantee means the warmth lands on the two
   things the ICP research says actually close this buyer.

**The one thing worth stealing from A**: the diagram plates. B fixes the hero diagram
with a warm pool, but the sprint diagram lower down still sits on bare ground, and A's
plate treatment is strictly better there. Folding A's `.plate` into B is about six
lines and no new concepts — say the word.

**C is worth keeping, not shipping now.** It's the most distinctive and the most
defensible if this practice ever needs to look institutional — but it's the wrong bet
before a segment is confirmed, because its whole value is a reference that only pays
off if the buyer really is handling financial instruments. Revisit it if the 30-day
test confirms bookkeeping. If the test kills #1 and trades becomes the segment, C is
the variant to drop and B still works unchanged.

## One base defect found and fixed in all three

Rendering at phone width — not reading the source — caught a bug that is **present in
`sm-site-b.html` itself**, not introduced here. Below 640px the base bumps the diagram
label size to 22 SVG user units so labels stay readable, but they're anchored at
`x=134` in a 560-unit viewBox and "Supplier orders" needs ~165 units. The overspill
was being clipped by the SVG viewport, so every phone rendered **"plier orders"** and
**"eekly report"**.

Fixed with `@media (max-width:640px){ .diagram{overflow:visible} }` — the spill costs
about 18 CSS px at 360px, which fits inside the 22px gutter. Carried into all three
variants. **`sm-site-b.html` still has it** if that file goes anywhere.

## Verification

Rendered in Chromium via Playwright, screenshotted at 1440 and 390, plus a
non-screenshot pass at 360.

- **No horizontal scroll** at 1440 / 390 / 360 on all three (`scrollWidth ===
  clientWidth`). B initially overflowed by exactly 20px at 390 — the hero figure's
  warm pool used a −12% horizontal bleed which, on a full-width 346px figure, pushed
  past the viewport. The bleed is now gated to the ≥960px two-column breakpoint.
- **Contrast**: automated pass over 17 text roles per page, sampling each element's
  computed colour against the nearest painted background. **Zero results below
  4.5:1** on all three. Key pairs: bone `#F2EDE3` 12.3:1, muted `#93A6A7` 5.65:1
  (4.77:1 over B's warmest wash), brass `#D2A968` 6.6:1.
- **Reduced motion**: emulated `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`; no content stays
  hidden on any variant. The only sub-90%-opacity elements are the two `.wire` groups,
  which are 50% opaque by design in the base. No treatment adds animation — every
  background here is a static gradient or static SVG.
- **Self-contained**: no external stylesheets, fonts, scripts or image URLs; no
  raster; no data-URI. All imagery is inline SVG or CSS gradient. Sizes: A 34KB,
  B 35KB, C 77KB.

Screenshots: `v2-{a,b,c}-{desk,mob}-{full,hero}.png`, `v2-{a,b,c}-pricing.png`.
