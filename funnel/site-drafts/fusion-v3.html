The work around the work.

Repetitive follow-ups. Information copied between systems. Documents that need sorting. Status checks. Reports rebuilt by hand. Tasks that depend on someone remembering the next step.

Individually, they seem small. Across a business, they consume hours, create errors and keep good people doing work software should be handling.

That is where I look first.

Fair questions.

Why can I not just use ChatGPT myself?

You can, and for some things you should. The gap is not the writing. A chat window answers when you open it. It does not run on Tuesday at six in the morning, it does not read the inbox, it does not stop and ask you when it is unsure, and it never tells you whether it saved you anything.

That plumbing is most of the work. Deciding which job is worth the plumbing is the rest of it, and that decision is what the session is for.

What if AI is not the right answer?

Then I say so, and the report says so. Plenty of these jobs are solved better by deterministic automation, by a setting in software you already pay for, or by changing who does what. AI is one option on the table, not the conclusion I start from.

A session that ends in “do not build this, do that instead” is a session that did its job.

How do you handle sensitive information?

Security and privacy requirements are part of the design rather than an afterthought to it. Which tools, permissions, data flows and AI providers get used depends on how sensitive the workflow is, on the systems you already run, and on the requirements you have to meet. Where AI should not have access to particular data, it does not.

The specifics come out of looking at your systems, so that is a conversation in the session rather than a paragraph here.

How do you know when it is done?

We define acceptance criteria before deployment, test the workflow against real scenarios, document the system, and agree on any post-launch support before implementation begins.

No system is perfect. That’s why we define acceptance criteria, test against real scenarios, and keep human approval wherever judgment matters.

Do I need to be technical?

No. You need to know the job well enough to tell me when an answer is wrong. That judgment is the only expertise the build depends on, and it is exactly why there is an approval step instead of a black box.
