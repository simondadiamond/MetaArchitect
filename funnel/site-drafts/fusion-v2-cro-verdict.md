# CRO verdict on fusion v2 — 2026-08-17

The CRO seat (Hormozi operating philosophy), asked to stress-test v2 adversarially.
It read `practice-plan-v1.md`, `icp-v2.md`, `fusion-v2-notes.md`, `fusion-v2.src.html`
and v1 for comparison. **It disagrees with most of v2's offer architecture.** Nothing
below has been applied to any page — these are Simon's calls.

## Ruling 1 — publish two prices, after rebuilding the second product

> "v2 didn't hide a price. It dissolved a product, then correctly noticed a
> product-less thing can't carry a price."

v1 sold a **Workflow Sprint**: one workflow, up to two integrations, a fourteen-day
live run, a measured result, a handoff. Bounded, defensible. v2 replaced it with "AI
Implementation" and six capability nouns — inputs, not outcomes. Not an offer, a
services page.

Why the outside reviewer's "C$1,750 reads less premium" argument fails:

- **Wrong buyer.** ICP #1 is by construction the *un-tooled* slice — a 1–20 person
  practice on QBO/Xero and email. They are asking "is this a $500 thing or a $50,000
  thing", not running an enterprise-delivery plausibility check.
- **Wrong constraint.** Nobody has refused any price of Simon's, because nobody has
  been quoted one. Optimising perceived premium-ness while the binding constraint is
  *zero conversations* is the error that produced the dead five-rung ladder.
- **The number is not implausible.** Aplos publishes $2k–$5k for a single workflow;
  Solas $2,500 per sprint; all 24 Zapier Solution Partners examined sit in the
  $1,001–$5,000 band. C$1,750 sits just under a real published band. *(Sourced from
  ICP v2 §2 — published asks, not proven transactions. No Quebec-local pricing
  exists; that lane was the fabricated one.)*
- **Opacity is charged to the scarcest resource.** "Discuss a project" spends
  Simon's four protected outreach hours answering what one line of text answers free,
  forever, at 2am.

Restore the Sprint, priced, with a pressure valve: *"Larger builds and multi-workflow
work are quoted separately."* Scope boundaries protect the anchor; hiding the number
does not. The real risk at C$1,750 is delivering sixty hours for $29/hr — that is a
**delivery scope** problem, fixed by the ceiling, not by opacity.

**And charge for the founding rate.** Simon is giving away C$750 and collecting
nothing. Exact page wording:

> **Founding rate.** The first three implementations are C$1,750 rather than C$2,500.
> In exchange I ask for one thing: permission to write up what we built and what it
> changed, with your name on it. If you would rather stay anonymous, the price is
> C$2,500 and I will not ask again.

## Ruling 2 — the guarantee comes back, bounded

> "Defined scope. Clear success criteria. Human oversight where it matters." is a
> description of professional competence. Three noun phrases every consultant claims,
> none of which move one dollar of risk off the buyer.

The suspicious thing in v1 was never *that* there was a guarantee — it was that "I
keep working at no charge until it is" **has no ceiling**. Fix the bound, don't delete
the instrument. Two supporting points:

- The "relevance beats risk reversal" finding (63.3% "not applicable" vs 6.7% "too
  expensive", BTOS; 40.0% vs 10.6% StatCan — verified primary data) is a ruling about
  **cold outreach**. Someone reading the offer section already cleared the relevance
  gate. Don't let an outreach finding demote a page element.
- Guarantees do the most work where nothing else signals quality. Zero testimonials,
  zero case studies, no sample report. Removing the only risk-reversal instrument when
  it is the only instrument is self-harm.

**Also bring back the parked "This practice is new" block** — it is the direct answer
to *"why does he need to promise that?"*

**Session guarantee — exact words:**

> **The guarantee.** You leave the session with a written report you can act on — with
> or without me. If you finish it and do not believe it was worth C$350, say so and I
> refund it in full. If my honest answer turns out to be "do not build this," that
> still counts as the session doing its job, and the report will say so in writing.

*(v1's "you leave with at least one workflow worth building, or you do not pay" makes
Simon eat the fee precisely when he does the most honest thing. It punishes integrity
and contradicts the page's own FAQ.)*

**Implementation guarantee — exact words:**

> **The guarantee.** Before I start, we write down what "working" means — the
> acceptance criteria, in your words, in writing. At the end of the fourteen-day live
> run the workflow either meets them, or I keep working at no charge until it does,
> for up to thirty further days. If it still does not meet them, the balance is waived
> and you owe nothing more.
>
> The deposit covers discovery and is not refundable. Once you accept the work there
> are no refunds, because labour cannot be un-delivered, and I would rather write that
> here than bury it.

Conditional, bounded, defined remedy — and it turns v2's slogan into the guarantee's
load-bearing mechanism.

## Ruling 3 — keep the session paid; the missing free step is the qualifying call

Keep C$350. The 30-day kill test measures **booked paid sessions** — making it free
destroys the instrument. A free front end burns the five weekly delivery hours on
people who were never going to buy. Paid diagnostics with credit-back exist in the
category (PointWake $497/45 min credited; Solas $800; Kopf $99 — sourced, all asks).

But the front end is the *email*, and the first yes is a short qualifying call —
free, and already mandatory in his process (TaxDome/Karbon → the deal doesn't exist).
It lives in the sales process and is invisible on the page. That is the actual gap.

**No lead magnet, no email capture.** Traffic for 90 days is ~40 people whose
addresses he already owns. Building capture for addresses he has is project-stacking.

**The one asset worth building is the sample report** — ungated, attached to the
outreach email, because its job is proof, not capture. Built on a workflow he actually
ran, provenance labelled honestly.

## The single biggest reason the page fails to convert

> It asks a stranger for C$350 with the lowest perceived-likelihood-of-success of any
> page Simon has published — no proof, no guarantee, no sample, no named buyer — so
> the only thing carrying the sale is Simon's own assertion that he is good at this.

Across the value equation, every v2 change but one moved the wrong way: dream outcome
vaguer, perceived likelihood collapsed (guarantee, live run, founding-rate honesty all
out), time delay worse (the phase durations that told people how long things take are
gone), effort worse (the buyer must compose an email from scratch). The one genuine
gain: *"nothing here assumes the answer is AI."*

Plus:

- **The fourteen-day live run is not an open question — it is the offer.** Nothing in
  the fetched competitor set publishes a live run with a measured result before
  acceptance. It is the one structural differentiator, and it is what makes the
  guarantee affordable rather than reckless.
- **No price says "plus applicable taxes."** GST/QST registration at C$30K. A buyer
  who agreed to C$1,750 and gets an invoice for ~C$2,012 feels ambushed by the person
  selling reliability. Add it before the first invoice.
- **v2 dropped US$.** Plan v1 keeps both published. But drop the line "prices are the
  same number in Canadian and US dollars" — stating the parity makes an American
  notice they are paying ~40% more.
- **Do not raise the session to C$500 yet.** Changing price while running the segment
  test confounds the result.

## "AI Implementation / Discuss a project / no price" — a dead end?

> "Yes, and it's worse than a dead end — it's a trap for the most valuable visitor on
> the page."

It asks for the largest commitment (an open-ended sales conversation) while offering
the least information — an inverted commitment gradient. Beside a card that *does*
show a price, priceless reads as *expensive, call us*. Replace the CTA with the
qualifying call his process already requires:

> **See if your workflow fits — 20 minutes**
> A short call to check the fit. If it isn't a fit, I'll tell you and it costs you nothing.

If Simon keeps implementation priceless, **delete the card entirely** — a capabilities
card with no price and no actionable step teaches the visitor that the answer to "what
does this cost" is "we won't tell you."

## Two things nobody asked about

**1. Every CTA is a `mailto:`. All six.** For an offer whose premise is *"I remove the
coordination work from your life,"* making the purchase itself a coordination task is
self-refuting. It also destroys measurement: the 30-day test decides a segment's fate
on booked sessions, and there is no booking mechanism and no event between "arrived"
and "an email showed up eventually." When the test returns one booking, Simon will not
be able to tell a dead segment from a broken page. **Highest-leverage fix on the list,
and it is a transaction mechanism, not a design opinion.**

**2. The hero de-narrowing is the page's largest strategic contradiction.** ICP v2's
post-mortem on v1 is that a description fitting every small business in North America
was unfalsifiable. *"I help businesses find where AI can save time…"* reinstalls that
framing at the top of the page. Survivable on one condition: **outreach must never
link here.** If the forty bookkeeping emails land on a narrow `/practices` page, the
broad homepage is harmless. If they land here, the research was wasted. Nothing in the
plan says which page the outreach links to — *"a decision with more revenue riding on
it than all three questions he asked me."*
