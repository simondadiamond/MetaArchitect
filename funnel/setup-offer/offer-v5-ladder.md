# Offer ladder v5 — CRO pass on the Aug 2026 positioning brief

Scope: §2 (offer mechanics) and the risk/payment structure. Positioning (§1) untouched
by request. Every number below is untested — this is a hypothesis to take into the 25
owner conversations, not a decision.

---

## What changed from the brief, and why

**1. Training moves to Phase 1.** The brief's timeline says first revenue is a
subcontracted training delivery at month 3–4 and $3–5k/month by month 6–9. That's
training income. But the Phase 1 ladder had no training rung — it appeared only in
Phase 3. The ladder and the timeline were describing different businesses. Training is
now rung 1, priced now, because it is both the first dollar and the warmest lead source
available: a room of owners who have watched you teach for two hours is a better prospect
list than any cold email.

**2. Funding is an objection-handler, not a hook.** Correction to the earlier CRO note.
Research on the 1% Act at 30 (enavantmath.org, 2026) finds that *a large proportion of
businesses currently subject to the Act would probably provide the required levels of
training even without it* — 85% of $1–2M payroll firms already reported training in 2014,
before they were subject. The unspent-pot premise is wrong. Firms over $2M are largely
already spending their 1%, with existing providers.

Consequence: the sale is displacement, not discovery. Which is exactly why §5 of the
brief is the stronger play — the CEGEPs, mutuelles and training firms already receiving
that budget are channels to supply, not incumbents to displace. Funding belongs on a page
and in the room when price comes up. It does not lead.

**3. No struck-through anchor.** Conceded: every price here is new, so singling out
$6,000 as unearned while blessing $4,500 was inconsistent. The fix isn't dropping the
anchor — it's using a credible one. Anchor against the alternative the buyer would
actually price: a dev shop quoting a custom internal tool, or the cost of the hire they'd
make instead. That comparison is real, verifiable, and makes $4,500 land without
inventing a former price.

**4. Unconditional refund on the Working Session.** Reinstated 2026-08-18 at Simon's
call. It costs close to nothing — a half-day that produces a plan the owner doesn't rate
should be refunded on the merits anyway, and you keep everything you learned about how
their business runs either way. What it buys is a stranger's first yes at zero risk,
which is the hardest yes in the whole ladder.

## Phase 1 — Founding (now)

| Rung | Offer | Price |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Discovery call, 20 min, behind a 3-question form | Free |
| 1 | Workshop / training half-day | $1,500 direct · $1,200–3,000/day subcontracted |
| 2 | Working Session (half day, written plan) | $750 — unconditionally refundable, and credited in full toward a build within 60 days |
| 3 | Build | $4,500 founding rate, first three only |
| 4 | Run & Improve | $500/month, offered at handoff |

**The founding exchange** (rung 3): written public case study, a reference call, and
permission to publish before/after numbers. The counter on the site decrements for real
or it doesn't exist.

**Waive rung 2 for workshop-sourced clients.** Someone who sat through your training has
already shown you how their work happens — charging them $750 to map it again is friction
on a warm lead. Cold and direct pays it; partner-sourced doesn't.

## Risk structure

**Working Session: unconditional refund.** If the written plan isn't worth what they
paid, they say so and the $750 goes back — no conditions, no deliverables returned. Say it
plainly in the offer; a refund nobody can find isn't risk reversal.

**Payment terms are risk reversal that costs no promise.**

- Build: 50% deposit, 50% on acceptance against a written spec. Never 100% up front from
  a stranger; never 100% on completion.
- The spec is the contract. Written before the deposit, agreed in writing, and it defines
  what "done" means.

**30-day spec warranty.** Anything that doesn't match the written spec gets fixed free
for 30 days after handoff. This is a warranty, not a guarantee — it answers the fear that
actually kills the sale ("he builds it, it's wrong, I'm out $4,500") without promising an
outcome that depends on their adoption.

**Explicitly not offered:** money-back on delivered build work (labour can't be
un-delivered), and any performance or ROI guarantee (adoption isn't in your control).

## Phase gates — unchanged from the brief

Proof, not dates. Three published case studies → Phase 2. Roughly eight engagements plus
one real before/after number → Phase 3. Don't advance early, don't stall past the gate.

## Standing constraints carried forward

No published hourly rate, ever. Recurring priced from day one. Every number here is a
hypothesis until 25 owner conversations say otherwise (§7 rule 2).
