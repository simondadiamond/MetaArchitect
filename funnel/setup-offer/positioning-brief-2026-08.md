## 1. The core decision

Sell **custom internal systems for Quebec SMEs, built in weeks instead of quarters**,
plus the training so their team can run it.

Not "AI consulting." Not Claude workspace setup. Not enterprise LLM engineering.

| Layer | What it is | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| 1 — AI operations | Tool wiring: n8n, Make, Claude Desktop, MCP, agent orchestration | Component of delivery and teaching content. Not the offer. Being commoditized by platform vendors. |
| **2 — Fast custom systems** | **Real internal software built fast because AI collapsed the cost. Quoting tools, intake portals, reporting pipelines.** | **This is the business.** |
| 3 — LLM systems engineering | Production model integration, eval harnesses, retrieval infra, guardrails. STATE territory. | Not the offer. Long sales cycle, credential-gated. |

**Differentiator:** six years of enterprise development (C#/.NET, React, SQL Server)
including tech lead work, on top of the AI tooling. Everyone will have the tooling. Few
will know what a correct system looks like — which is what allows speed without shipping
something that dies in month three.

**The client is not buying AI.** They are buying software they previously couldn't
afford. AI is the cost structure, not the pitch.

### Why this is the right call

- It's what he's already been doing for two years — not a pivot into an adjacent field.
- It's the work he likes: come in, set up, build fast, ship.
- The front half of every engagement is discovery and mapping how work actually happens,
  which is a genuine strength rather than a tax.
- Teaching is a funded revenue line, not a side project.
- It's fundable — every part maps to an existing Quebec program (§4).
- He's comfortable with it. A positioning he believes is one he'll still be running in
  six months.

### STATE framework

Keep it. Move it. It is the credibility artifact and the content engine — the reason a
fast build isn't a cowboy build. It lives on the blog and in the room. **It does not
appear on the pricing page.** It also keeps the Layer 3 door open at zero cost.

free discovery call → $750 Working Session (credited toward a build within 60 days) →
$4,500 founding build (first three, in exchange for a written public case study) →

### Rules

- **Phase gates are proof, not dates.**
- **No published hourly rate, ever.** Bounded fixed-fee products only.
- **Recurring is the goal.** Project revenue resets to zero every month.
- **Price the support layer from day one** or end up owning it for free.

### Working Session — positioning copy

> Half a day. We map how one job actually gets done today, decide what's worth building,
> and you leave with a written plan specific enough to hand to somebody else. Credited in
> full if we build it together within sixty days.

One paid step before the build, not two.

### Discovery call

Free, 20 minutes, two fixed windows a week, behind a three-question form. At this stage
the calls are market research as much as sales.

> "Free, twenty minutes, no deck. I'll tell you honestly whether this is worth your
> money. If it isn't, we stop there."

## 4. Funding

Funding is the answer to "Quebec buyers are cost-conscious." They are cautious with
discretionary spend and behave differently when the money is mandatory or reimbursed.

**Correction (2026-08-18, verified):** the unspent-pot premise is wrong. In 2020 there
were 9,589 employers subject to the 1% Act and **88.7% declared investing at least 1%**.
They know about it and they already have suppliers. Funding is an **objection-handler,
not a hook** — it belongs on a page and in the room when price comes up. It does not
lead. The one timely angle: the rules **loosened in April 2026** to admit more informal
training and simplify the process. That is four months old and most employers haven't
absorbed it.

### Loi du 1% (Loi sur les compétences, D-8.3)

Employers with payroll over $2M must spend at least 1% of payroll on eligible training
or pay the difference to the FDRCMO via Revenu Québec. Declaration due by Feb 28.
CPMT accredited-trainer status makes the invoice trivially countable
(agrement.formateur@mess.gouv.qc.ca). Routes: 135h pedagogical training, OR 250h training
experience, OR 90h + 100h. **Not a blocker** — delivering through a CEGEP or university
requires no accreditation at all.

### MFOR — Mesure de formation de la main-d'œuvre (Services Québec)

Covers firms under the $2M threshold. Generally 50% of eligible expenses, bonified to
**75% for new-technology training**. Cap generally $100k per agreement. Employee wages
reimbursable to $25/h. No deadline, continuous intake via a conseiller aux entreprises.

### ADRIQ — Offensive Tr@ns Num

Up to 125 hours of consulting, 50% of costs to a $25,000 max. Covers process
modernization, automation, software selection, AI/IoT/data, CRM. Accreditation is an
application, not an exam. Contact: Lyne Morin, lyne.morin@adriq.com, 418-573-0782.

### Federal

DEC (Québec regions): AI adoption and commercialization, up to 90% of project cost,
opened June 2026. PARI-CNRC: advisory plus non-repayable contributions. Neither requires
consultant accreditation — the client applies, Simon is the supplier.

### Stacking

One client, two funded envelopes: build funded by Tr@ns Num / ESSOR / DEC, training
funded by MFOR or their 1% obligation. Two revenue lines, one recurring.

## 9. Guardrails on any CRO / offer-optimizer pass

Run it on §2 and §3. Keep it off §1.

- **No urgency theatre.** No fake scarcity, no countdown timers, no "3 spots left" that
  never decreases. The founding-slot counter must be real and must decrement.
- **No income-claim register.** Selling the dream of the business is wrong for Quebec SMB
  buyers and risky for someone with a day job and a local reputation.
- **No price inflation ahead of proof.** Phase 1 is deliberately underpriced. That's a
  case-study acquisition cost, not an error to correct.
- **Steps, not jumps.** Phase gates exist so pricing rises with evidence.
- **Voice stays spartan.** Short sentences, active, no hype.
