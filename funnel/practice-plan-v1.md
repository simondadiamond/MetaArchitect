## Why this plan replaces the productized ladder

1. **Platform commoditization.** Commercial agent platforms (Grok, Claude Cowork) now
   ship agents, scheduled tasks, triggers and mobile access at subscription prices.
   Anything sold as an interface gets eaten on a vendor's release cadence. The durable
   value is workflow selection, implementation judgment, integration and measurement —
   never owning the surface. Tool-agnostic positioning is the structural answer.
2. **Zero delivered clients, zero social proof.** The v4 ladder had five priced rungs,
   two deep-research passes and 639 lines of spec, and closed nothing. An offer at this
   stage must be sized for a first yes, not for a mature practice.
3. **Scope reality.** One workflow, not an OS. No templating, no multi-workflow build,
   integrations frequently being an MCP hookup measured in minutes. Priced accordingly —
   the previous flagship priced a scope that no longer exists.

Carried forward from the prior estate: the outcome-sentence gate, no fake scarcity,
published prices, and the Simon-minutes value rule.

## The one sentence

Simon takes one piece of recurring work the owner personally coordinates and turns it
into a reliable AI-assisted workflow — using whatever tools fit, with human approval
where judgment matters.

## Positioning

Tool-agnostic by design. Not a platform reseller, not a Claude shop.

Working headline: **"Stop being the glue holding your operations together."**
(Beats "Turn AI into the way your business works" — it names the buyer's felt state,
it's falsifiable, and it came out of real ICP language. Final wording is the CMO seat's
call, pending the ICP refresh.)

## The offer — three things

### 1. Workflow Mapping Session — C$350 / US$350

90–120 minutes plus a written plan: the workflow mapped, value and feasibility
estimated, a recommended implementation. Credited in full toward a Sprint booked
within 30 days.

**Guarantee:** you leave with at least one workflow worth building, or you don't pay.

### 2. Workflow Sprint — founding C$1,750 (first 3) / standard C$2,500

One recurring workflow, live in the business. Up to two integrations. A human-approval
step wherever judgment is required. Short written handoff. Fourteen-day live run with
a measured result.

**Terms:** 50% deposit to start. Balance due on acceptance after the 14-day run.

**Guarantee (the part that was missing):** if the workflow isn't doing the job at the
end of the fourteen days, Simon keeps working at no charge until it is — or the balance
is waived. The deposit covers discovery and is not refundable. No refunds after
acceptance; labour can't be un-delivered, and saying so plainly is part of the offer.

**Price-raise trigger:** after three delivered Sprints with a measured result, standard
moves to C$4,000 and scope grows to two workflows. Founding pricing ends at three
clients — not a countdown, a real capacity fact.

### 3. Ongoing Improvement — C$400–600/mo

Post-Sprint only, bounded scope and response times. Never sold before a Sprint ships,
and never treated as the exit vehicle.

Everything else — multi-workflow builds, workshops, templates, cohorts — is quoted case
by case and stays off the website until it has been delivered at least twice.

## First 90 days — what actually produces revenue

**Conversations per week is the only leading indicator that matters. Target: five.**

Where they come from, in order of warmth:

1. The 17 untouched warm CRM rows — one burst, roughly two weeks of supply.
2. Sym — website at C$1,750 with a 50% deposit, then the workflow conversation it opens.
3. **Bookkeeping and accounting practices** — reclassified 2026-08-14 by the ICP v2
   research (`funnel/icp-v2.md`). This plan originally listed them as a referral channel
   *to* owner-operators. The evidence says they are the buyer, and a channel second.
   Sourced from the QuickBooks ProAdvisor and Xero Partner directories, geo-filtered to
   Quebec and Ontario — public, free, finite, and it surfaces the exact stack the Sprint
   targets.
4. Referral partners: Phil Ross (Dempton), Anthony Nadon (Alpaga IA), plus MSPs and web
   agencies around Quebec City. One good partner outperforms months of content.
5. Sym's network after delivery — property managers and short-term-rental owners. Note
   that property management dropped out of the ICP v2 ranked list entirely; Sym stays a
   paid door and a case study, not the template client.
6. Local rooms: chamber, BNI-shaped groups.
7. Content — real, but lagged 6–18 months. Not counted in this window.

**Disqualifying question, asked before any proposal:** does the practice already run
TaxDome or Karbon? Both ship automated document chasing out of the box. If the answer is
yes, there is no Sprint to sell — the buyer is the firm on QuickBooks or Xero plus email
with no practice-management layer, which is precisely why they are the ones complaining.
Filter these out at list-build, before they ever reach a call.

**The ask in every one of those is the C$350 mapping session**, never "do you need AI help."

Approved: a Claude-built automated social + newsletter pipeline, specced once by Simon

## Quit trigger (aggressive-but-evidenced)

All of:

- C$7,500–8,000 collected per month, averaged over four months
- ≥ C$25,000 signed or highly credible backlog
- ≥ 3 clients bought the core service; ≥ 1 is not a friend or relative
- 6–9 months of household expenses in cash
- No client above ~50% of projected revenue
- Simon can name where the next ten qualified conversations come from

Planning number: roughly **C$120K annual revenue ≈ C$80K salary replacement**, assuming
80–85% operating margin, a proper tax reserve and no employees. GST/QST registration is
generally required once worldwide taxable supplies pass C$30K. Separate accounts from
day one: sales tax collected, ~35% net-profit tax reserve, operating, personal. Have a
Quebec accountant produce the household-specific number before resigning.

## What is still unvalidated — say it out loud

- **The ICP.** Superseded 2026-08-14 by `funnel/icp-v2.md`: #1 is now bookkeeping,
  accounting and tax practices of 1–20 staff running QuickBooks Online or Xero with no
  practice-management layer, chasing missing documents every month. Confidence is
  **moderate and self-declared as falling**, because the research also found TaxDome and
  Karbon already ship this automation. Trades and field service is the specified fallback
  test, not a dead segment — the tension there is real and stated: trades showed the
  loudest pain but has the lowest consultant-use rate in the economy (2.2% vs 6.9% for
  accounting). Pain intensity is not purchase probability. That confusion is what broke v1.
- **Whether the segment buys at all.** The 30-day test: 40 Canadian bookkeeping practices
  with TaxDome and Karbon users filtered out at list-build, one named workflow, bilingual
  personal email, asking for the C$350 session. ~4 Simon-hours, C$0 cash. Zero or one
  booking kills the segment and triggers the trades test rather than a copy rewrite.
- **Price level, and it now points up.** Comparable diagnostics run higher than C$350 —
  PointWake at $497 for 45 minutes, Solas at $800 for a 14-day prototype. Quote C$500 to
  half the outreach list as a free parallel experiment. Nobody has refused any price yet
  because nobody has been quoted one.
- The sharpest hook found in research — *"With Hubdoc gone, my workflow is completely
  broken"* — is **unverified**. Confirm with Xero before it appears in any outreach.

## Decision rules (priority order for any recommendation)

1. Paid client conversations
2. Delivering measurable client value
3. Testimonials and case studies
4. Repeatable offer development
5. Reusable delivery systems
6. Content derived from real work
7. Free tools or product development

Challenge any build not required to close or deliver the next client. Automate only
after the underlying task has been done manually at least twice. Prefer narrow
experiments and reversible tool choices.

delivery hours · effective revenue per delivery hour · measured client value ·
testimonials · referrals · ICP-matched subscribers · calls generated by content.

Not tracked: followers, stars, post volume.
