## Script (point form + narration lines)

### 1. Cold open (0:00–0:45)
- SHOT: screen recording, the morning brief render. Slow push on "Nothing needs you this morning."
- NARRATION: "Anthropic just shipped 15 ready-to-run workflows for small businesses. Free, if you're already paying for Claude. I installed everything, connected my tools, and asked for my briefing. It said: 'Nothing needs you this morning.' I have a client to close, a video to film, and a business to build. Something needs me. Stay with me, because what happened here is not a bug. It's the most useful thing you can learn about AI and your business this year."

### 2. What it is (0:45–2:00)
- SHOT: claude.com plugin page, connector logos.
- BEATS: launched May 2026; a plugin inside Claude Cowork (desktop app); 15 command workflows on 15 building-block skills; connectors: QuickBooks, PayPal, HubSpot, Canva, DocuSign, Google Workspace, M365, Slack. Not installed by default: marketplace → Install → toggle connectors.
- NARRATION anchor: "If you pay for Claude, this costs you nothing. Install it. I mean that. And keep watching, because free is exactly why you need to know where it stops."

### 3. Setup walkthrough (2:00–4:00)
- SHOT: real install + `/smb-onboard` flow, connector toggles going green. Timelapse the waiting.
- BEATS: OAuth per connector; pin the workflows you'll use; it runs one recipe end-to-end so you see value early. This section SERVES the "how to use claude cowork" searcher; keep it genuinely instructional.
- HONESTY BEAT (on camera, verbatim from the run): it told me my account "looks more like a personal/dev setup than an active small business." NARRATION: "It's honest about its own blind spots. That's the best thing about it, and I want you to remember this sentence, because it's about to matter."

### 4. The voice setup (4:00–5:00)
- SHOT: the three onboarding questions on screen.
- BEATS: it configures your writing voice with three questions: overall tone, how drafts should be formatted, signature habits. They're preference picks, not writing samples. Simon's actual answers (use on screen): tone = spartan; format = bullets when useful; habits = short sentences, active voice.
- NARRATION: "Three questions. I answered honestly: spartan, bullets when useful, short sentences. Here's what it never asked: to read anything I've written. It configured my voice without hearing it. Hold onto that, because three checkboxes is roughly the size of the gap we're about to walk into. Your voice isn't three answers. Neither is your business."

### 5. The run (5:00–6:30)
- SHOT: `/monday-brief` output, real and unedited (sanitized figures if any real ones appear — real financials never on camera).
- BEATS from the verified run: Cash — QuickBooks not connected, skipped. Sales — PayPal shows only transfer pairs netting to zero. Pipeline — one deal in HubSpot: the built-in sample, stalled since February. The one real thing it flagged: a failed GitHub job.
- NARRATION: "So my briefing knows my accounting software is empty and my CRM has a sample deal in it. The one real thing it caught was a developer alert. It found the business it expected. It didn't find the one I have."

### 6. The turn (6:30–7:30)
- SHOT: talking head, or slow scroll over the connector list.
- NARRATION: "Here's the important part. This is not a broken product. It's a worldview. This product believes your business lives inside QuickBooks, HubSpot, and PayPal. Some businesses do. But if you sell your expertise — if you're a consultant, a coach, a solo professional — your business lives somewhere else. It lives in conversations. In proposals you half-finished at 11pm. In what you decided about your prices last month and already forgot. No connector reaches that. And Anthropic knows it: they deliberately left the business-context layer for you to build. The container ships empty because every business fills it differently."

### 7. The reveal (7:30–9:00)
- SHOT: Simon's real Monday brief, same morning. Then one draft going out in his voice.
- BEATS: it knows the prospect and what stage they're at; it knows the deadline; it knows last month's pricing decision and doesn't re-ask; the follow-up draft sounds like Simon because it's built from everything Simon has written, with checks a draft has to pass before it ships — not three preferences.
- BRIDGE (verbatim): "What you're watching is three years of compounding. You don't start here. I didn't start here. You start with one rung: a workspace that knows your clients, your prices, and your voice. And then it grows."

### 8. Close (9:00–9:45)
- NARRATION: "So: should you install Claude for Small Business? Yes. It's free, it's careful, and the briefing habit alone is worth it. But then you'll hit the same wall I did, and you'll learn the real question. It's not which AI you use. It's who teaches it your business. If you want yours set up around the work you already do, that's what I do. Link below. And if you connect QuickBooks and it tells you nothing needs you this morning — check. Something needs you."
- CTA on screen: simonparis.ca/setup — one line, no urgency theatrics.

## Filming notes

- First take is allowed to be rough; edit pass is Claude's job.
- Never fabricate demo data. Sample/sandbox data must be visibly labeled. Real financials never on camera.
- Read the plugin's honest disclaimer on camera — it is the anti-bashing insurance.
- Decline the plugin's offer to schedule its recurring Monday brief (we keep ours); optional on-camera beat.
- The plugin may improve (a context layer could ship later). Nothing in the script claims permanence; the close ("who teaches it your business") survives any feature release.
