**Rules that go with it:**

- **One accent, and it marks a number or a commitment — never a label.** Prices,
  guarantee lines, the primary CTA fill. Not eyebrows, not section numbers, not links,
  not bullets. ~6–8 uses per page is the ceiling. A mark that repeats becomes a texture.
  (Stitch's v2 currently uses umber for the dark card's list bullets — that violates
  this and should be pulled back to paper or muted.)
- **No green third tone.** Petrol *is* the green. A legible green lands near `#2F6B58`,
  close enough to petrol to read as an inconsistency and far enough to read as a second
  brand colour, and there is no unowned role for it — sales pages have no success states.
- **No metallics, no gradients, no glows, no pill buttons.** All buttons sharp-cornered.
- **Warm grounds only.** If a product screenshot ever lands on a warm band it will pick
  up a yellow cast; put it on a pure-white inset card with a hairline `#102E33` at 12%,
  so the white reads as the artifact rather than a palette break.
- Style name that produced the good output, keep using it with Stitch: **"quiet luxury"**
  — restraint, air, typography, no ornament.

## 4. What is settled, and by whom

- **Light theme, warm paper ground, one uniform base** — Simon's own rule from the
  gradient review: every section shares the same base background, so there are no seams.
- **Type**: editorial serif display against small letterspaced uppercase labels, warm
  humanist sans for body. `atelier-a` uses Libre Caslon Text + Hanken Grotesk. Not
  Playfair, not Cormorant. **The type scale must be dramatic** — ~60px against ~12px,
  nothing in the middle doing duty as a section title.
- **White space is the primary material.** 110–130px between sections. This is not
  taste: the one controlled test located (n=1,193) found white space and typeface are
  what move perceived quality — colour and finish did not.
- **Vertical-neutral main page + per-vertical landing pages.** Approved by Simon this
  session. See §6.
- **Two things to buy, not three.** C$350 mapping session and C$1,750 sprint. Ongoing
  improvement (C$400–600/mo) is post-sprint only, so it is one quiet line below the two
  cards — never a third tier, and never an "Enterprise / Custom / Contact" rung.
- **Copy is frozen** at `atelier-a`'s wording until the 30-day segment test resolves
  (prior handoff §4). Stitch's copy is placeholder in every round — discard all of it.

6. **Remove the invented logo/seal** beside the wordmark. The wordmark is Simon's name

## 6. The landing pages

Architecture Simon approved: **one neutral spine, swappable evidence blocks.**

- `/` — neutral, no named vertical. Its job is to make a cold, sector-unknown arrival
  say *"that's my job"* and hand them onward. Traffic: LinkedIn, referral partners, name
  searches. It must NOT carry vertical loss-math, must not name a software stack, and
  must not try to close C$350 alone.
- `/practices` — bookkeeping/accounting/tax. **Build this one first**; it is the segment
  the 30-day test targets. Language from `icp-v2.md` §3: *unmatched transactions,
  month-end, missing statements, the receipt chase*, and the campaign line
  *"It isn't really a list, it's a feeling you get at 11pm."*
- `/field` — trades and field service, the designated fallback. *Quotes going cold.*

The sector-bearing surface is small by design: **six SVG input labels, four problem
paragraphs, one hero line.** Everything else — design, guarantee, pricing, diagram
geometry — is shared. This also produces the landing copy for the §6 ICP test.

**Why this matters, and it is the sharpest finding of the session:** ICP v2 lines 42–43
(verified, BTOS + StatCan) show the dominant objection is *relevance*, not cost —
"not applicable to this business" 63.3% vs "too expensive" 6.7% in the US; 40.0% vs
10.6% in Canada. A deliberately vertical-neutral page optimises against the objection
that does not bind. Also note `atelier-a`'s current copy is quietly **trades-flavoured**
(supplier orders, "the quote that needs your numbers", "a contractor chooses materials"),
so sending bookkeeper outreach to it is a message-match break.

## 7. Verification standard — non-negotiable

Render in Chromium via `playwright-core`; do not reason about it.

- **Audit contrast against actually painted pixels**, sampling the ink as the extreme
  painted pixel at 3× and the ground as the modal painted pixel. Sampling the *computed*
  colour hid two real failures this session — `.proc-n` was ink at `opacity:.2`, painting
  at 1.28:1, and the older `--bone-3` miss has the same root cause. **Audit the pixel,
  not the token.**
- No horizontal overflow at 360, 390, 768, 1024, 1180, 1440.
- Reduced motion: every reveal painted, every diagram drawn.
- Zero network requests outside `file://` and `data:` — the artifact must be
  self-contained (fonts and images base64-inlined).
- Watch the `padding` shorthand on any element that also carries `.wrap`: it silently
  zeroes the side padding and dumps text against the phone's edge. Use `padding-block`.

## 8. Where the CMO comes in

Copy fine-tuning is the CMO seat's, not sitemaster's — but **not yet for the main page**.
Main-page copy is frozen until the segment test resolves. Bring the CMO in for:

- **`/practices` copy**, once the fusion lands. That is a known buyer in known language
  and it is a strong use of the seat.
- **The guarantee rename** — "The C$350 guarantee — you leave with at least one workflow
  worth building, or you don't pay." Same terms, quantified before it's read (stolen from
  Solas's "The $1,000 guarantee"). This is CRO territory, not CMO; needs Simon's sign-off.

Three CMO research lanes ran this session. Findings that bind future work:

- **Trust:** guarantees work *best* where there are no other quality cues (Ostrom &
  Iacobucci) — which is exactly Simon. But guarantees are rare in this category (2 of 7
  competitors), and rarity plus an unknown seller invites the "why does he need to
  promise that?" inference (Wu/Liang; magnitude unverified). Net: keep them **scoped,
  specific and mechanically collectable**, never unconditional.
- **The "no client logos" disclosure** is defensible as two-sided messaging (Eisend),
  NOT as pratfall or blemishing — those require low-effort processing and a peripheral
  flaw, and a C$2,500 decision is neither. Three rules follow: keep it short, place it
  *after* the positives, and pair it immediately with the compensating cue. It currently
  sits below the price with "that narrowness is the reason the guarantee can be this
  specific" as the next line. **Leave it there. Do not promote it up the page.**
- **Published pricing** is the best-evidenced element on the page — buyers' #1 wish-list
  item four years running; 6 of 7 competitors publish. Simon's numbers sit inside the
  observed band (Ovamind $500/$2,500, Solas $800/$2,500, PointWake $497).
- **Colour**: no located evidence for metallics→wealth, or for any colour effect in B2B
  professional services. The claims circulating on agency blogs ("blue +42% trust in
  professional services", "red CTAs +31.4% per Baymard") are untraceable or contradicted
  by their own cited source. Accent hue is a null variable versus contrast. Do not
  re-open the palette on a colour-psychology argument.

## 10. How Stitch behaves, so you don't relearn it

Five rounds of evidence. **Short prompts beat long ones, decisively.** The best output
came from ~40 lines: a style name, the palette, who is buying, and a list of sections
with the *goal* of each and no execution detail. The worst came from ~120 lines of
prohibitions — banning every source of visual mass produced a page with no focal point,
an outlined ghost button, and a truncation before the pricing section so the accent
colour never appeared at all.

Rules that hold:

- Give it a **style name** it knows ("quiet luxury"), the palette, the buyer, and the
  sections-with-goals. Let it choose execution.
- Tell it **its copy will be replaced** so it does not treat words as the deliverable.
- **Never ask for empty slots.** Two labelled empty rectangles destroyed the composition
  — a design tool cannot compose around content it cannot see. Let it draw a placeholder
  and swap afterwards.
- Long prompts cause it to **truncate**, and it truncates from the bottom, which is where
  pricing lives.
- It reliably re-inserts: stock photographs of people, invented company names ("Solo AI"),
  logo seals, neutral grey bands, and rounded buttons among square ones. Check for all
  five every round.
- Revision prompts work well when they are a short numbered list of changes that opens by
  saying what to keep.
