# Client-acquisition channels for the /setup venture — deep research verdict (2026-07-21)

> Deep-research run (102 agents, 20 sources fetched, 89 claims extracted, 25 adversarially verified: 13 confirmed / 12 refuted). Question: is LinkedIn commenting/content the right primary path to 3 founding clients ($5K) in 60–90 days, and what channel mix is?
> Full output: workflow wf_528d7c05-988 (session 240f32ca). This doc is the durable record.

## Verdict

**The evidence does not support LinkedIn content/commenting as the primary path to 3 founding clients in 60–90 days.** Every surviving case study of comparable solo operators attributes first clients to warm network, referrals, and communities where the seller already participated — never to cold channels or content reaching strangers. Commenting survives only as a visibility/warm-up mechanic; every quantified comment-to-client conversion claim failed adversarial verification.

## What survived (confirmed findings)

1. **First clients come from warm network + referrals + communities** (HIGH confidence, 3-0/3-0/2-1 votes). Marie Poulin (Notion Mastery): first customers inbound from Twitter + bootstrapped-founder Slack groups. Charlotte Isaac (Dubsado/ops consultant — closest ICP analog): built almost entirely on referrals ("friends told friends"). Fractional-leader surveys: 84% find first client from network; ~93% acquire via network referrals. Caveat: ~1 in 6 first clients still comes from outside the network.
2. **LinkedIn organic reach is materially shrinking** (HIGH). ~47–66% decline across three independent datasets (van der Blom 1.8M posts, AuthoredUp 3M+, Saywhat 329K); same-creator frequency cap in feed. 4–5 posts/week is top of recommended range, not penalized — but volume cannot compensate for a zero-audience start.
3. **Daily posting alone does not produce clients on this timeline** (HIGH). 100-day experiment (Ferdinand Goetzen, from an 8K-follower base): 270K+ impressions, 872 followers, "a handful of leads", zero reported paying clients.
4. **Commenting = visibility mechanic only** (MEDIUM). Substantive >10–15-word comments do increase visibility (van der Blom: 15+ word comments ~2x ranking weight). But NO verified comment→client conversion evidence exists — the exact step our engage plan depends on. Every quantified conversion claim (Fullfunnel's 61 deals, "3x more inbound", comment-to-like ratios, 15-min-beats-an-hour) was refuted 0-3 or 1-2.
5. **Cold email is the worst-fit channel** (MEDIUM). Belkins 2025, 7.5M emails: 0.45% average reply rate. Volume game incompatible with 3 high-trust $5K sales.
6. **Engagement warm-up before DMs is directionally supported** (LOW — vendor-only evidence, Expandi 70K campaigns; lift not isolated).

## Caveats

No surviving claim comes from a neutral primary dataset about the exact ICP; closest analogs are Notion/ops consultants and fractional execs. "First clients from network" partly reflects survivorship among people who had networks. The commenting-conversion gap is absence of surviving evidence, not proof of absence. Algorithm data is practitioner-collected (LinkedIn publishes none); van der Blom sells LinkedIn training.

## Strategy implications (COO read)

1. **Lead with warm-network activation**: sister/client #0 as referral engine, direct personal outreach to existing contacts, adjacent-provider partnerships (OBMs, VAs, bookkeepers, business coaches).
2. **Community participation** where the ICP gathers — requires discovery first (open question #1).
3. **LinkedIn demoted to support layer**: maintenance posting cadence (credibility surface for referral checks, not growth engine); commenting re-aimed from "big creators' comment sections" toward warming tracked PROSPECTS' own posts before DM (the one outbound mechanic with directional support — and what prospect-mine + CRM already enable).
4. **Cold email: out.**
5. Engage-queue operator-lane build (goal dee67686): still useful but re-scoped toward prospect warm-up, and sequenced AFTER warm-network motion is running.
