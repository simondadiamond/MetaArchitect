## Step 2 — Patterns

**Promises that repeat**: replace a team member / VA; stop re-explaining your business every session; one workspace instead of ten tools; save hours on admin; sounds like a real business, not a template.

**Problems people are clearly paying to solve**: scattered ops (CRM + invoicing + content in one place — Notion OS cluster); generic AI output that doesn't match their voice; not knowing how to configure Claude/ChatGPT for their specific business; too many prompts, not enough of a system.

**Most common formats**: (1) Notion "Business OS" templates, (2) ChatGPT/Claude prompt packs, (3) Skool/Whop communities with a course + prompt library, (4) DFY bundles at the top.

**Price clustering — the actual finding**: a barbell, not a smooth curve.
- $5–$29: commodity prompt packs, high volume, low trust, low perceived value.
- $39–$99: the "Business OS" template sweet spot — the most credible packaged one-time product tier found (Hashim's Business OS, Company OS, Notion AI Mastery Hub).
- $47 lifetime / $29–$99/mo: community access (Skool, Stan Store) — recurring, not one-time.
- $150–$700: DFY bundles and bespoke packs.
- **$97–$299 as a one-time packaged SKU is thin.** Almost nothing found sells cleanly in that exact band as a single fixed-price, self-contained deliverable — sellers either underprice at $39–99 (template-only, no AI layer) or jump to $150+ bundles/DFY, or convert to recurring ($47–99/mo). This is the gap, and it lines up with the actual gap in Simon's own ladder: nothing sits between Sessions ($125/hr, unbounded scope) and the Audit ($2,500).

**Hooks used to sell**: team-replacement framing; dollar-figure "behind the scenes" reveals; review-count/star flexing (88 reviews, 10,000+ customers); "one document/one workspace fixes everything."

**Objections buyers likely have**: "I already pay for Claude/ChatGPT, why buy a template" (Anthropic's free Claude for Small Business bundle makes this sharper, not softer); "is this generic or will it actually fit MY business"; "did this person actually build a business or do they just sell courses" (trust gap — sharpened by how much of this category is being pirated/resold on warez sites, which signals real demand but also a credibility-starved market).

**Gaps still open**:
1. Nobody in this exact niche runs the "boring evidence" play — every comp found leans on hype/wealth-signaling (Dan Harrison's "$50K/month," "Wealth Warp"). Zero reliability/receipts framing exists here — this is the same gap the Aug 3 operator-channel research found industry-wide, confirmed again at the product level.
2. The $97–299 one-time-SKU band is underbuilt relative to demand at the $39–99 and $150+ tiers around it.
3. Nothing productizes "get your business context set up so Claude already knows it" as a fixed-price single deliverable — every comp is either a static template (no live delivery) or a full course/community (ongoing commitment). That exact middle is empty.

## COO framing note

Every idea above sits **between** Working Sessions ($125/hr) and the Audit ($2,500) on the existing ladder — none of them is pitched as a parallel, unrelated SKU. That's a deliberate constraint: the Aug 3 Acuña masterclass research (`docs/research/operator-channel-research-2026-08-03.md`) landed on "pick three offers, do not add rungs" as a standing guard, and this research does not override that. Read this doc as answering "what would a packaged, fixed-price version of the existing offer look like, and is there market evidence anyone wants it" — not as a pitch for a fourth/fifth SKU. A decision to actually build and sell one of these is Simon's call, not something this research resolves on its own.
